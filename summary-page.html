Verifying zeta-23

On 13 August 2026, Alpöge and Furman published a proof, found by an AI system and formalized in Lean 4, that more than two thirds of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function are simple and lie on the critical line. It is unrefereed and, so far as we can determine, no completed independent verification. Parts have been checked publicly by others — a kernel rebuild and axiom audit, and a reproduction of the ceiling data from the published enclosures — and this report corroborates those. What it adds is a statement-level audit, which no other effort has attempted, and an independently constructed witness. With transcripts and registered criteria, rather than assurances.

How to read this

Each finding below shows three things: the hypothesis and pass/fail criteria that were written down before the check ran, the verbatim output of the check, and the verdict. That ordering is the point — a verification whose criteria are chosen after seeing the result isn't one. Every artifact was checksummed when it was produced; the checksums are listed at the bottom and reproduced in the repository.

Registered before running Pass: “all recomputed values verifiably inside their claimed enclosures.” Fail: “any recomputed enclosure verifiably violated.” Outcome: inconclusive — the defining data is not published, so the primary check could not be performed at all.

The law itself lives in a file that is in neither the repo nor the arXiv source:

The paper (§7.2), the source header above, and the repository README all state this accurately and consistently; the finding is about availability, not disclosure. Its consequence is that the ≈0.682 ceiling — unlike Theorems A–E — is not reproducible end to end by a third party. The main theorems do not depend on any of this, as finding 01 confirms.

Written from the Lean semantics in Python with exact integer arithmetic, parsing the enclosure integers straight from the source. One incidental observation supports the enclosures' provenance: 131 of the 255 rows deviate from the idealised grid value by exactly one unit in the last place (2⁻¹⁴⁰), with irregular per-row rounding — the fingerprint of a real high-precision interval computation rather than synthetic data.

05 An independently constructed witness certifies the same kind of ceiling with every ingredient public. New artifact

p = 0.681810782

= 412129329181963620513241 / 604462909807282866662336 · τ = 1/10⁷ · 255 rows enclosed in 260-bit interval arithmetic

A witness law caps the method class, so any feasible law gives a valid ceiling — and a lower one is a stronger result. Ours was built from scratch by column generation over unrestricted configurations (0.859793 → 0.795583 → 0.749218 → 0.682435 → 0.681811), then certified with exact rational weights and rigorous enclosures. It sits below the paper's 0.681828687 by 1.79 × 10⁻⁵, satisfies the paper's own edge bound |D(1)| ≤ 0.82395317, and nothing in it is private — checking it needs no search:

What this does and doesn't say

It establishes

  • The proofs check, on hardware unrelated to the authors'.
  • The formal statements mean what the paper says they mean.
  • The main theorems are provably independent of the unverifiable certificate.

It does not

  • Make any new claim about the zeta function.
  • Certify the informal mathematics beyond what a kernel and a careful reading establish.
  • Claim our witness is optimal, or that the published ceiling is wrong — our construction is consistent with it.

Further limits, stated in the report: we did not re-run the external comparator tool (statement equality was established by byte-identity plus a kernel-unfolding argument); the ξ′ and PairCeiling comparator statements passed the axiom audit but their fidelity was outside our registered scope; and Mathlib's semantics for riemannZeta and analyticOrderAt are trusted as the declared base.
